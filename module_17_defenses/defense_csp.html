Module 17 · Defense

Content Security Policy (CSP) defense

CSP is a browser-enforced capability policy delivered as a response header. It doesn't stop injection; it stops the injected content from executing or exfiltrating. It is the most important defense that works even when your encoding has a gap.

How it works

The server sends a Content-Security-Policy header listing what the page may load and run, by directive. The browser enforces it and reports violations. The XSS-relevant directives:

DirectiveControlsCloses mechanism
script-srcwhere scripts may come from; whether inline/eval are allowed1, 2 (inline handlers), and javascript: in 3
object-src 'none'<object>/<embed> plugins1, 3
base-uri 'none'<base> hijackingdangling-markup / relative-URL abuse (M08)
connect-src / img-src / default-srcwhere the page may send/fetch dataexfiltration (M11, Magecart)
form-actionwhere forms may submitdangling-markup theft (M08)
require-trusted-types-for 'script'forces Trusted Types on DOM sinks4

The critical distinction: 'unsafe-inline'

A CSP with script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline' is almost worthless against XSS — it re-permits inline <script> and inline event handlers, i.e. mechanisms 1 and 2. Many "we have a CSP" deployments are exactly this and provide little protection. See it yourself:

▶ policy=loose (unsafe-inline) — the payload still runs vs ▶ policy=strict — blocked (check the console).

Nonce-based / 'strict-dynamic' CSP (the modern recommendation)

Allowlisting hosts is fragile (bypassable via JSONP endpoints, open CDNs). The current best practice is a nonce-based policy:

Content-Security-Policy:
  script-src 'nonce-RANDOM' 'strict-dynamic';
  object-src 'none';
  base-uri 'none';
  • Every legitimate <script> carries nonce="RANDOM" (a fresh, unguessable per-response value). Injected scripts lack the nonce and are refused — closing mechanism 1 regardless of how the payload is spelled (Module 12 obfuscation becomes irrelevant).
  • 'strict-dynamic' lets a nonced script load further scripts it trusts, so you don't need a brittle host allowlist.
  • Inline event handlers (mechanism 2) are not covered by a nonce — they are blocked because 'unsafe-inline' is absent. Good.

Try it: /defense/csp-search?policy=nonce — the injected inline <script> has no nonce and is refused.

What CSP does NOT do

  • It does not prevent the injection — the malicious markup is still in your DOM; it just can't execute. Combine with encoding.
  • A misconfigured CSP (unsafe-inline, wildcard hosts, open JSONP in the allowlist) can be bypassed. Test it.
  • DOM-based XSS via eval needs 'unsafe-eval' to be absent; DOM sinks like innerHTML are only fully closed by Trusted Types (next page).
  • Data exfiltration can sometimes leak via DNS/prefetch even under connect-src; scope default-src tightly.

Deployment tips

  • Start in report-only mode (Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only) to find violations without breaking the site.
  • Prefer nonces + 'strict-dynamic' over host allowlists.
  • Always add object-src 'none' and base-uri 'none'.
  • Layer require-trusted-types-for 'script' once your DOM sinks are Trusted-Types-clean.

Checkpoint
