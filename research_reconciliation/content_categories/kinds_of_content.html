Kinds of Content & the Euler Diagram

Seven categories, but they're not seven separate buckets — they overlap and nest. Getting the nesting right is the difference between "guessing what can go where" and knowing it.

The relationships (this is the key picture)

The spec draws these as nested sets. In words:

  • Sectioning, Heading, Phrasing, and Embedded are all kinds of Flow. Flow is the big outer set — most body content.
  • Embedded is also a kind of Phrasing. So an <img> is embedded and phrasing and flow, all at once.
  • Metadata mostly stands apart (head-level), though a few elements (script, noscript, scoped style) are both metadata and flow.
  • Interactive cuts across flow (e.g. a, button, input).

Embedded sits inside phrasing, which sits inside flow. Interactive (dashed) is a cross-cutting category. Heading and sectioning are flow but not phrasing — that's the block-level boundary.

Why the nesting is the useful part

🧠 Brain Power
A <p>'s content model is "phrasing content." Given the diagram, can a <p> contain an <img>? A <div>? (Yes to <img> — it's phrasing (and embedded). No to <div> — it's flow but not phrasing. That one rule explains "why can't I put a div in a p.")

Two footnotes the spec insists on

  • Inter-element whitespace — spaces/newlines between elements are always allowed and are ignored when checking a content model. (Relevant to parsers and to "why does my formatting not break conformance.")
  • Multiple membership — many elements are in several categories at once, sometimes conditionally (an <input> is interactive unless type=hidden; an <a> is phrasing only if it contains only phrasing content). Those conditionals are where transparent content (page 5) comes in.
