Sectioning & Heading Content

These two categories exist to answer one question: "what is the title of this region, and how deep is it?" They're flow content, but a special kind — the structural skeleton of a document.

Sectioning content — scopes for headings and footers

The spec defines sectioning content as content "that defines the scope of headings and footers." Just four elements:

ElementMeaning
<article>a self-contained, independently distributable composition
<aside>content tangential to the surrounding content
<nav>a section of navigation links
<section>a generic thematic grouping

Each one starts a new sectioning scope: headings inside it belong to it, not to the document as a whole.

Heading content — the header of a section

Heading content is <h1>–<h6> plus <hgroup>. A heading "defines the header of a section — whether explicitly marked up using sectioning content, or implied by the heading content itself." That parenthetical is the interesting part: a bare <h2> with no surrounding <section> implicitly starts a section.

🧠 Brain Power — the document outline
In this draft's model, sectioning + heading content together produce a document outline (like a table of contents the browser could compute). An <h1> inside a nested <section> is a sub-heading, not a top-level one, regardless of the number "1". (Modern HTML has since walked back the auto-outline algorithm — accessibility tools never implemented it — so today you should still use h1–h6 levels meaningfully. Good example of a spec idea that didn't survive contact with implementations.)

Note: sectioning & heading are flow but not phrasing — so they can't live inside a <p> or <span>. That's the block boundary again.

Security relevance (smaller, but real)

  • Sectioning/heading elements are structural and generally safe to allow in rich-text sanitizers — they carry no URL/script/embedded surface.
  • But they interact with DOM clobbering (Track A3): injecting <section id=…>/<nav name=…> still creates named references. Structural ≠ inert with respect to naming.
  • <hgroup> and outline quirks are mostly a correctness/a11y concern, not a direct XSS vector.
