Content Models · Head First · a Track B deep-dive

Data, CDATA, RCDATA & PCDATA — Oh My!

Four almost-identical words that quietly decide whether your <script> is a tag or just text. They confuse everyone — so let's stop memorizing definitions and instead build one mental model you can run in your head for any snippet, forever.

Case File · why this module exists

The sanitizer that passed every test and shipped an XSS anyway

A team writes an HTML sanitizer. It correctly strips <script> from normal content. It ships. Weeks later: stored XSS. The payload? A comment that put markup inside a <style> tag. The sanitizer "saw" inert text; the browser, on a second parse, saw a live <img onerror>. Nobody on the team could have predicted it — because nobody could name the content model the bytes were in. By the end of this module, you will name it on sight.

The one mental model: two dials

Forget the four scary names for a second. Every run of text the parser reads has exactly two dials, and a content model is just a setting of those two dials:

The TAG dial
🏷️
Does < start a tag,
or is it literal text?
The ENTITY dial
&
Does &amp; become &,
or stay literal?

That's it. Two on/off switches. The "four confusing words" are just the four useful combinations:

Content modelTAG dialENTITY dialWhere you meet it
DATA (a.k.a. PCDATA)ONONnormal content — <div>, <p>, body text
RCDATAOFFON<title>, <textarea>
RAWTEXTOFFOFF<style>, <xmp>, <iframe>
script-dataOFF*OFF<script> (RAWTEXT + a maze of side-doors)
PLAINTEXTOFF 🔒OFF<plaintext> — and the dial jams forever
CDATA sectionOFFOFF<![CDATA[…]]> — but ONLY inside <svg>/<math>
The single sentence that unlocks it all: "CDATA" just tells you the result is text — it says nothing about the ENTITY dial. That's why RCDATA (entities on) and RAWTEXT/CDATA (entities off) are both "just text" yet behave differently. The word you were told to key on was the wrong one.

Which content model am I in? (run this in your head)

  1. Look at the nearest enclosing element. It, not the bytes, picks the dials.
  2. Is it <title> or <textarea>? → RCDATA (tags off, entities on).
  3. Is it <style>/<xmp>/<iframe>/<noembed>/<noframes>? → RAWTEXT (both off).
  4. Is it <script>? → script-data (both off, plus escape sub-states).
  5. Are you inside <svg>/<math> and see <![CDATA[? → a real CDATA section.
  6. Otherwise → DATA. Both dials on. This is where injection becomes markup.
🔥 Fireside Chat — the content models argue about who's safest
DATA
I'm the honest one. What you write is what you get. You typed <b>? You get a bold element. That's not my fault attackers love me.
RCDATA
Careful — you're where XSS happens, DATA. I'm subtler. Your <b> is just text to me… but I still decode &amp;. People forget that half and get the escaping wrong.
RAWTEXT
Amateurs. I take everything literally — tags, entities, all of it. You think that makes me safe? It makes me a trap: you can't entity-escape your way out of me. One stray </style> and you're back in DATA's arms.
CDATA section
And I'm two-faced on purpose. Inside SVG I'm a real fortress of literal text. Out in plain HTML? I collapse into a "bogus comment" and people's payloads leak right past me.
DATA
…so we all agree the danger isn't which of us is "safe." It's a human assuming the wrong dial setting.

There are no Dumb Questions
Q: Aren't PCDATA and DATA literally the same thing? Why two names?
A: Effectively yes. "PCDATA" is the old SGML name; HTML5 renamed the concept "the data state." Same dial setting (both on). You'll see both words in the wild; treat them as synonyms.
Q: If the element decides the content model, how does an attacker ever "switch" models?
A: They don't switch the dials directly — they close the current element early (a stray </title> or </style>) so the following bytes land back in DATA. That "break-out" is the whole attack pattern, and the Playground lets you test it.
Q: Is this the same "content model" as the other module (metadata/flow/phrasing)?
A: No — different meaning entirely. This module = how text is tokenized (tags/entities on or off). The Content Categories module = which elements may contain which. Same phrase, two worlds.

Key points
  • A content model is just a setting of two dials: TAG (does < open a tag?) and ENTITY (does &amp; decode?).
  • DATA = both on · RCDATA = tags off/entities on · RAWTEXT & script & CDATA-section = both off.
  • The enclosing element chooses the dials, not the bytes.
  • "CDATA" only means "the result is text" — it says nothing about the ENTITY dial. That's the core confusion.
  • Attackers don't flip dials; they close the element early to fall back into DATA.
  • The right escaping depends on the model — which is why one-size-fits-all encoding fails.
